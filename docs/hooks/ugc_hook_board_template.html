Read this before you brief anything

  • These are winners with no losers. Every source hook beat its own creator's baseline — the median by about 4×. You can see what working hooks look like; you cannot conclude one angle beats another, because the failures were never sampled.
  • Lift is creator-relative, not raw views. The lift figure is views ÷ that creator's own baseline, so a big account gets no free credit. That is the one thing this data does better than every published "average views by hook type" study.
  • Direct-sell angles are thinly evidenced here. Only 5 templates each survive for the sell and proof angles. The corpus skews to curiosity and relatability, so treat the sell rows as leads.
  • Anything marked "indicative" has under 10 uses. Those rank high on lift precisely because small samples swing. Sort by independent creators when you need to be sure.
  • The source runs to January 2025 — roughly 19 months old. Hook patterns decay as they saturate, and the data already shows the most common angles performing worst. Read these as proven structures, not current trends.
  • Shoot format is inferred from wording. No video was watched. A "how to" line is assumed to need a screen demo; verify before you build a brief on it.
  • English-dominant, with no Nepali or Hindi coverage at all — the gap that matters most for Baakhapaa, and one this source cannot fill.
