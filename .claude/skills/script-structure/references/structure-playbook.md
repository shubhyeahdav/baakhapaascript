# Structure Playbook — distilled from all analyzed sources

Every technique below comes from a `knowledge_base.json` analysis. Techniques
transfer; plot surfaces don't. Never reference source titles in generated
output.

## 1. Shorts grammar (15–90 s)

Beat spine: **hook (0–3 s) → escalation → core payoff → optional twist → soft CTA.**
Every beat carries a retention function: stop_scroll, open_loop, payoff,
rewatch_trigger, share_trigger. A second with no retention function is where
viewers leave.

Hook types (pick ONE and commit): pattern_interrupt · bold_claim · question ·
visual_shock · relatable_pain · curiosity_gap.

Category patterns:

| Category | Opening move | Middle | Ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| Educational | Counterintuitive claim stated as fact in 0–3 s | Stakes ("why this matters to you"), then ONE concrete mechanism | Callback to the opening claim + soft CTA |
| Storytime | Cold-open at the crisis moment mid-story, then rewind | Compressed setup escalating back to the opening moment with new context | Payoff exceeds or subverts what the open implied; twist before CTA |
| Transformation | After-state flashed in the first frame as proof | Compressed process with visible incremental wins | Side-by-side comparison + one actionable takeaway |
| Comedy skit | Absurd premise inside the FIRST line of dialogue | Rule of three: same joke escalated 3×, each beat shorter | Third escalation breaks the established pattern; no CTA — the loop rewatch is the CTA |

## 2. Scene / movie grammar

Beat spine: **setup → inciting incident → rising tension → crisis → resolution**
(three-act time split ≈ 33/33/34). For every beat, be able to answer:
- What dramatic question does it pose or answer?
- What changes? (state of the world, a relationship, what a character knows)
- What are the characters NOT saying? (subtext guidance)

### Technique library, indexed by the problem you're solving

**Weak inciting incident / passive protagonist**
- Make the inciting opportunity something the protagonist *wants*, so the
  audience becomes complicit and every win raises the cost of exposure
  (Korean thriller pattern).
- Make each act of pursuing the goal cost the mentor/protagonist something
  material, so progress itself carries stakes (Bollywood underdog pattern).

**Saggy middle**
- Structure the middle as infiltration/accumulation: each small victory
  compounds risk; a literal or tonal descent at the midpoint flips the genre
  register (comedy→dread) (Korean thriller pattern).
- Let the investigation be a trail authored by the antagonist — the
  protagonist's progress IS the trap tightening (Korean revenge pattern).
- Distribute disappointment across many small scenes instead of one betrayal;
  meaning accumulates through routine and what is pointedly unsaid
  (Japanese slow-cinema pattern).

**Flat antagonist**
- Make the true antagonist institutional (family expectation, an education
  system, class) so individual authority figures stay human and even
  sympathetic (Bollywood pattern — the Baakhapaa default).
- Or a "respectable" figure whose menace escalates quietly beneath social
  politeness (Malayalam pattern).

**Unearned or predictable ending**
- Redefine victory as self-respect rather than winning, so the ending
  satisfies even when the protagonist loses the surface contest
  (Hollywood underdog pattern).
- Converge all ensemble arcs at one physical place; heal a broken group one
  pairwise relationship at a time, each unlocking the next (Malayalam
  ensemble pattern).
- Detonate the climax at a celebration — the least safe "safe" place
  (Korean pattern).
- Resolution as acceptance rather than change; a late off-screen loss
  reframes every earlier scene (Japanese pattern).

## 3. Web series grammar

Scene grammar PLUS two episode-level fields:

**episode_hook_type** — the mechanism that drives the next-episode click:
revelation · imminent_danger · impossible_decision · unexpected_arrival ·
betrayal · unanswered_question. Setup/midpoint episodes must end on their
stated hook; finales are exempt (they owe resolution instead).

**season_arc_position** — setup / midpoint / finale changes what an episode
owes: setup episodes plant; midpoint episodes pivot the protagonist's goal or
self-image; finales pay debts and may withhold the thing the protagonist
originally wanted, revealing they no longer want it.

Tradition patterns:

- **TVF-style (weight this heaviest — closest to Baakhapaa's voice):** ground
  each episode in ONE mundane, hyper-specific problem (a batch change, a lost
  notebook, a broken solar light). Relatability is the hook, not plot. Episode
  shape: small practical problem → escalation through peer/institutional
  friction → a mentor reframe or humane resolution → quiet emotional beat.
  Episode hooks are unresolved *anxieties*, not cliffhangers. Season arc = an
  attitude change (contempt → investment; innocence eroding), not an event.
- **K-drama:** make the obstacle structural (a border, a duty, a contract) —
  never a misunderstanding — so intimacy grows through small care rituals
  while the threat stays external. Alternate episode hooks between
  romantic near-misses and external danger. Separate before the reunion.
- **Western prestige:** let the style itself be the arc (e.g., a narrator
  device the story eventually turns against the narrator). Reveal a withheld
  trauma in fragments; detonate it in the penultimate episode; the finale
  strips the protagonist's armor.

## 4. Baakhapaa house application

Default emotional engine: **ambition vs family expectation, modern Kathmandu.**
The institutional-antagonist pattern is the house default — parents and
teachers are pressure carriers, not villains. Dialogue carries subtext through
code-mixing: characters switch between Nepali and English when deflecting vs
being sincere. Endings should follow the earned-understanding pattern
(self-respect redefinition, pairwise reconciliation) — never total triumph or
total tragedy, both read as melodrama here.
